Research

Fairness and interpretability in AI.

Published · NHSJS, April 2026Gold CREST Award

Fairness and Dependency Preservation in Synthetic Credit Data

Can synthetic data safely stand in for real data in loan approval models? I compared SMOTE, CTGAN, TVAE and Gaussian Copula on the German Credit dataset and created a Dependency Preservation Score to measure how well each kept the relationships between credit variables intact.

The finding: every synthetic method distorted those relationships and made fairness less stable, so for high-stakes decisions like lending, original data with fairness-aware preprocessing is the safer choice. The work earned a Gold CREST Award from the British Science Association.

Try the idea

A toy version of the question in my paper: does synthetic data keep the relationships between variables, and does fairness stay stable?

Toy illustration. This uses a small made-up dataset generated in your browser and simplified stand-in generators, not the German Credit data, the real methods or the paper's score. For the real results, read the paper.

How I built this site

The whole site is one HTML file with no build step. The 3D room, Mandalink island, the rooftop and Bangalore are drawn with Three.js, and every model (the furniture, the creatures, the weapons, the city) is built from simple shapes in code rather than downloaded. Sound effects are generated live with the Web Audio API, progress is saved in your browser, and other visitors show up as ghosts through a small public message relay. There is a simple version for screen readers and keyboards, a reduce-effects setting for slower devices, and I test it with a headless browser.
