# Season plans

The plan for all 15 seasons, plus ideas saved for later. Each season is four weeks with a Part a week, starting 18 November 2026 and cycling. Every season has its own original villain, an intro cinematic, weekly chapters and a finale. It gives you a tool or vehicle you keep, three skins and a permanent change to the map. See the Seasons section of `CLAUDE.md` for how a season file plugs into the page.

## The story bible (read this first; where a season's section below disagrees, this wins)

The seasons are one story, told a month at a time. Every season can be played on its own, but none of them is filler: each is caused by the one before, and each causes the next. Live games that tried a loose, villain-of-the-month structure (Fortnite's later chapters, Destiny's early seasons) lost their players' trust; the ones remembered are the ones where the threads connect.

### The spine in one paragraph
A Bangalore teenager survives the night a meteor lands, and spends the next two years learning that every time you save a city, somebody else pays for it. The meteor is a key, sent by the Keepers to lock a tear in the sky; turned the other way, it opens it. On the other side of the tear is {N} Prime: you, from a Bangalore where you sold the meteor's core on the first night and kept selling until there was nothing left to sell but other worlds. Prime feeds those worlds to the Collector. Every season's villain is somebody who found a way to profit from the tear's crisis, and every season's victory loosens the lock a little more, until Prime walks through. The ending (Season 15) asks you to be the one who pays.

### The theme: everyone pays
One idea runs through every season and every villain: **a debt**. Somebody always charges for survival.
- The Salvager sells meteor fragments (S1). Brine charges a toll for every roof (S2). Marigold sells "protection", and the ledger is literally a debt book (S3). Devi bills the city for its own power (S4). Cinder's cult makes offerings (S5). The Vashk demand tribute (S6). Ssera trades Earth for her people's safety (S7). Prime collects worlds (S8), his governors in New Bangalore extract data, land, food and water (S9–S14), and the Collector is the creditor at the end of the chain (S15).
- You are the only one who never charges. The question the saga keeps asking is whether that can last.

### The mythology, and when each piece is revealed
- **The tear** (seen from Season 1): a wound between universes that Prime keeps open. Seasons 1–7 show it growing.
- **The meteor is a key** (the Keeper says so at the end of S1: "keys are made to open things"; Vorrak explains it in S6). Turned one way it locks the tear; turned the other, it opens it. It isn't a weapon, which is why everyone who tries to use it as one makes things worse.
- **The Keepers** (S1's mysterious stranger; New Bangalore has its own in S10). The reveal, saved for S15: **every Keeper is a version of you, from a world where you chose to close the lock yourself.** Plants: the Keeper asks your name, then says "Remember that I asked" (she already knew); she hums the song from your room's speakers; she has a scar on the same hand you burn in S5; New Bangalore's Keeper is an old woman "with your eyes".
- **{N} Prime** (glimpsed in S7, arrives in S8): the road not taken. In S1 the Salvager offers to buy the core and you refuse; Prime is the version of you who said yes. He isn't a cackling villain. He believes collecting is mercy: worlds that are going to fall anyway, falling quickly.
- **The Collector** (named by Vorrak in S6, arrives in S15): the creditor. It doesn't hate anyone. It's owed.
- **The cycle**: the seasons loop after 15. That isn't a technical accident; it's the Keepers' price. Closing the lock resets the world, and someone has to stay behind to keep it shut.

### The people, and how they change
- **You ({N})**: Phase 1, someone who survives things. Phase 2, someone who starts over with nothing in a world that isn't theirs. The ending is a choice about whether you're willing to pay what everyone else has been charging.
- **Inspector, then Commissioner, Devi (S2–S4)**: the saga's most human villain. Introduced as a voice on the survivors' radio in S2, organising rescues while every official channel stays silent. She watched the government let the city drown, and decided the city needed someone who'd never let go of the wheel. She is kind to you, and means it, and uses you anyway. She never explains herself in a speech; her one real line of self-justification comes in S4, in a power station at 3 a.m., to nobody in particular.
- **Velvet (S3, S7)**: switches sides in S3. In S7 she's the first person a Mimic replaces, because walking out on Marigold made her visible. Freeing the real Velvet is the emotional centre of S7.
- **Arjun (S2, S3)**: your friend. It's his flat you're in when the wave hits (S2's cold open). Brine's crew pull him out of the water and put him on the ship's pumps; you find his voice note in a dry bag and free him in the finale. In S3 he's in Dubai, working off the family's debt to the Syndicate, and the Comet is his car.
- **Bhaskar (S2) and Lata (S3)**: brother and sister, Arjun's father and aunt; the city's ordinary people, who keep turning up: Bhaskar's camp becomes the shelter in S4's blackout; Lata's chai stall is where the resistance meets in S7, because a Mimic can fake a face but not her tea.
- **Mr. Ravindran, the Ledger (S3)**: the man who kept the books. He comes back in S4 as the only person who can read Devi's accounts, and he trades them for a new name.
- **Dr. Neel (S5–S6)**: studied the core before anyone else, and builds the boost pack and the Kite from what he learned. He's the one who says out loud, late in S6, that every time they've won, it's been worse afterwards.
- **The Salvager (S1)**: his buyer, whom he mentions and never names, is Cinder (S5). He's the first person to offer you a deal, which matters in S8.

### How the story is told (rules for every season)
- **Show it in the world first.** Graffiti, posters, radio, what's on the shelves, which lights are on. A collector saluting Devi says more than any line of dialogue about her.
- **Every villain is in the season before their boss fight**, not just at the end of it: a voice, a name on a crate, a car that passes you.
- **Plant early, pay off late, and never pay off what wasn't planted.** Keep a running list (below) of every plant and where it pays off.
- **Each season reinvents the look of the whole site** (see Site themes below), so the world feels different before you've read a word.
- **Trailers are short films, not lists.** No name cards with quotes. Characters are introduced by how they're framed; the title arrives late; the last shot is an image you'll understand only at the end of the season.

### How Seasons 2 and 3 tell their stories (from research into games that do it well)
- **A relationship is the spine** (The Last of Us). S2 is about getting Arjun home; Bhaskar hands you his son's harpoon "for when he wants it back", and the season ends on the two of them at the foot of the camp building.
- **Quiet scenes between the fights** (The Last of Us). After each of S2's first three Parts, a short night at the camp fire: no exposition, just people (`evening` in `s02.js`). In S3, Devi brings you chai after Brass and talks about her father's cycle shop.
- **The world tells the story** (Subnautica). Dry bags float in S2's flooded streets, each holding a phone with a voice note from the night of the wave; one is Arjun's, and it's how you learn he's alive. The phone's Flock feed and Messages carry the city's reaction to every season.
- **The villain has a reason, early** (Spider-Man's Doctor Octopus). Brine's crew came through the tear from a world with no land left; the pumps end the only life they know. Devi is kind to you from the first scene, and her reason (her father paid men like Brass for twenty years) is the same reason she betrays you.
- **Cover costs something** (Sleeping Dogs). S3 Part 2 opens with an undercover day collecting for the Syndicate; the second stop is Lata's cart, and you choose whether to take her money or pay it yourself. She remembers, in person and by text.
- **Light beside heavy** (Yakuza). Shopkeepers you rescue text you afterwards; Lata's lines stay dry and stubborn even when the story isn't.

### Dialogue rules (no cringe)
- **No catchphrases, no villain monologues, no "you and I are not so different", no "catch me if you can".** If a line would fit on a T-shirt, cut it.
- **Subtext over statement.** People talk around what they mean. The information comes out sideways, during an argument about something else, or not at all.
- **Short lines.** One or two sentences. People interrupt, deflect, and leave things unsaid.
- **Every line does a job:** reveals who someone is, changes a relationship, or moves the plot. Lines that only explain the plan get cut, and the plan goes into the world.
- **Villains believe they're right.** Each gets a reason you could almost agree with, and nobody announces that they're evil.
- **No jokes during grief.** Humour comes from character (Lata's stubbornness, Bhaskar's bad radio), not from one-liners.
- **Names sparingly.** A character saying {N} should land, not decorate.
- **Bangalore is home, not flavour.** People talk like people from here; no tourist phrases dropped in for colour.

## The rules that hold for every season

- **Naming:** the seasons are grouped into phases, and a season is a chapter of its phase (e.g. Season 9 is Phase 2 · Chapter 1). Each season's four weekly sections are its **Parts**, 1 to 4. The built seasons (1 and 2) still say "Chapter" in the game; they'll be renamed to Parts when the game code is next touched.

- The seasons are loosely linked: each has its own story and you can jump in at any one. Light threads run through them all: the Keeper (a mysterious figure), the traces each season leaves on the map, and the tear in the sky.
- Every season changes the map, gives you one new tool or vehicle to keep, and leaves a permanent trace.
- Progress is saved in `ru-season` and your name in `ru-heroname` (default "Traveller").
- Real winter (December to early January) is a bonus layer on top of whichever season is running.
- All villains, aliens and skins are original: no Marvel or DC characters. Season 7's aliens are clearly fictional, with no real-world conspiracy theories.
- **Skins:**
  - There are three per season (45 in all):
    - a story skin (finish the story);
    - a challenge skin (complete the season's challenge);
    - a villain skin (beat the villain).
  - They are earned by playing, never bought, and kept for good (`ru-arsenal`).
  - You equip them from the Wardrobe on the phone.
  - A season currency can buy small extras such as colour variants.
  - Owning every skin unlocks a final one: The Keeper.
- **Still to decide:**
  - whether missed skins come back when the cycle loops;
  - whether a past season's fights stay replayable.

## Season 2 · High Water (16 December 2026 – 12 January 2027)

### Story

**Cold open (16 Dec).**
- A few days after Landfall, you're visiting a friend's highrise, high up and looking out at the sea, trying to move on from everything that happened.
- The glass starts to hum. The tear over Crash Isle splits wider, and the wall of water seen at the end of Season 1 pours through it and races across the sea.
- The wave hits the tower, the window bursts, and you're thrown out into the air. Black screen.
- **You wake up on a wooden plank in the middle of a drowned Bangalore.** Only the tops of the tall buildings stick out of the water, there's wreckage everywhere, and a fin is circling.

**Villain (original): Captain Brine.** A pirate who rode the wave in through the tear on a ship built from wreckage. To him a drowned city is the perfect harbour, and he means to keep it that way. His crew raid the rooftop survivors for supplies, and the sharks follow his ship like hounds.

**Chapter 1 · Adrift (16 Dec).**
- You paddle your plank between floating wreckage while sharks circle.
- Early on you find a battered boat caught in the top of a billboard: the tool you keep for the season and after.
- You reach a camp of survivors on the rooftops, linked by rope bridges, and find your way back towards your own tower.
- Boss: **the first great shark.** It hunts you across open water until you make it to the rooftops.

**Chapter 2 · Brine's Harbour (23 Dec).**
- Brine's crew raid the rooftop camp and take its supplies.
- You chase their raider boats through the drowned streets and fight on rooftops and decks to get the supplies back.
- Boss: **Brine's first mate**, fought on a raider boat.

**Chapter 3 · The Deep (30 Dec).**
- The survivors give you diving gear, and you go down into the drowned streets: cars, bus stops and the metro line under the water.
- You find the old floodgates and pumps that could drain the city.
- Boss: **a huge armoured shark** guarding the pump house, fought underwater.

**Chapter 4 · High Water (6 Jan, finale).**
- You start the pumps and board Brine's ship as the water begins to fall.
- The fight has three phases:
  1. A boat chase.
  2. Fighting on the deck.
  3. A duel up the mast as the water drops away beneath the ship.
- **The city drains.** One neighbourhood stays underwater for good: the sunken district.
- The ending leads straight into Season 3.

### The flooded city
- While Season 2 runs, the whole city sits under about 6–8 m of water: low buildings are gone, and towers become islands.
- **The whole map is swimmable**, both on the surface and under it. Through the clear water you can see the old streets below.
- Rooftops are safe spots, linked by rope bridges and planks.
- **Sharks** patrol, and a fin shows where each one is.
  - When one locks on it circles, then lunges, with a warning you can dodge.
  - Bites hurt, and a shark can knock you out: you wake up on the nearest rooftop.
  - You stop them with harpoons: a harpoon gun you get in Chapter 1 drives them off or brings them down.
  - Getting out of the water onto a rooftop or the boat keeps you safe.
  - Bigger ones turn up in deeper water.
- **Other dangers:** jellyfish swarms, whirlpools round collapsed buildings, and debris carried by the current.
- **Christmas crossover:** at night the shallow water between rooftops freezes into skating lanes, and the boat gets a light on the bow.

### What you keep
- **Vehicle:** the boat. **Tools:** the harpoon gun and the diving gear.
- **Skins:**
  - Diver: finish the story.
  - Canal Captain: the challenge, e.g. finish Chapter 1 without being bitten.
  - Brine's Coat: beat the finale.
- **Left on the map:** the sunken district.

### Engine work (in `index.html`, so later seasons can use it too)
- Crash Isle and the other Season 1 map changes move out of `seasons/s01.js` before 16 Dec, or they vanish when Season 1 stops loading.
- **A flooded-city mode:** one big water surface over the whole map, underwater fog and light, and the traffic and pedestrians switched off. The water level can fall for the finale.
- **Swimming and diving everywhere:** reuse the sea swimming, and add diving with a breath meter.
- **A walking height on building tops**, like the mountains have.
- **Boat physics.**
- **Sharks:** new enemies that swim, circle and lunge, built on the season fight engine.

## Season 3 · The Syndicate (13 January – 9 February 2027) ✓ built

### Story
While Bangalore was underwater, the police stations flooded, the phones went dead and nobody was in charge, so someone moved in. During the flood the Marigold Syndicate sold pumps, boats and "protection" to the rooftop camps. When the water drains, they own half the city. Every rebuilt shop pays them, their trucks run the streets, and their marigold stamp is on every door.

**The cast**
- **Madame Marigold:** head of the Marigold Syndicate. Elegant and polite, always with a fresh marigold in her lapel, and never seen getting her hands dirty. *“A city this beautiful needs someone to look after it.”*
- **Her three lieutenants:**
  - Brass, the enforcer;
  - the Ledger, the accountant;
  - Velvet, the getaway driver.
- **Inspector Devi:** an honest police inspector whose station is still full of mud. Officially she has no power left, so she works with you. Or so it seems.
- **The Keeper:** in the background. The Syndicate is looking for something that came through the tear.

**Cold open (13 Jan).**
- The morning after the drain, the city is mud and wreckage, and people are trying to reopen.
- You walk down your street and every shutter has a fresh marigold stamp.
- A collector in a cream suit tells a chaiwala his "protection" is due, and when he can't pay his cart gets tipped over.
- Inspector Devi pulls up in a mud-caked police jeep: *“My station is underwater and my phone is dead. You’re the only one in this city I’ve seen do anything. Want a job?”*

**Parts (one a week)**

| Week | Part | What you do | Boss |
|---|---|---|---|

One convoy is a decoy, and it's an ambush. | **Velvet**, in a high-speed chase on the highway. Beaten, she switches sides and tells you about the missing payroll page. |

**The fight you lose (end of Chapter 2).**
- You escape the counting house with the ledger and run straight into a trap: Brass and Velvet together. Then Madame Marigold steps out of the car.
- The fight can't be won. Brass's hits get stronger every round, and when you're on your last heart it stops.
- Marigold takes the ledger and pins a marigold on your jacket: *“Wear it. Now you belong to me too.”*
- You wake up in Devi's jeep. She *found* you, of course, and the ledger is gone.

**Plot twists.**
1. **The Ledger** is Mr. Ravindran, the tax inspector everyone thought had drowned. He faked his death to keep the Syndicate's books.
2. **Velvet switches sides** after Chapter 3. She tells you a payroll page is missing: the strongest hint at Devi, which most players will still miss.
3. **The tear connection:** Marigold's greenhouse is full of plants that came through the tear, and she's been selling them.
4. **Devi** in the finale.

**Devi's betrayal is only hinted at, never stated.**
- She always arrives just after the Syndicate has cleared out.
- She asks for the ledger "for evidence", and the page with the payroll is missing when it comes back.

# Phase 3 · Shifting Bangalore (from Season 16, Phase 3 · Chapter 1)

## Phase 3 · Chapter 1 (Season 16) · Warped (12 January – 8 February 2028)

### How it starts
- Season 15 ends with the lock closed and the world restarted, with you (or whoever paid instead) as the Keeper at the pier.
- But the restarted world isn't quite the old one. The Moon is still cracked from Season 6, and on the night of Landfall its pull bends the meteor's path by a few kilometres. The Keeper sees it happen and can't stop it. The meteor misses the sea off the west coast, and comes down instead on the **Kaveri Energy Research Facility**, a (fictional) experimental reactor on the edge of the city.
- The core and the reactor together don't explode. They **break space-time**.

### The world: a Bangalore phasing between realities
- **It's Bangalore again**, a new one in a new cycle. It won't stay put: the whole world **phases in and out of different realities**.
- Every few minutes there's a shimmer, and the city around you swaps for another version of itself. The streets keep roughly the same layout, so you always know where you are, but everything on them changes. Some of the realities it falls into:
  - **Prehistoric Bangalore:** jungle and swamp, giant ferns, and something large moving between the trees;
  - **Medieval Bangalore:** a walled fort town, a market of tents, guards on the walls;
  - **Far-future Bangalore:** gleaming towers, sky-traffic and silent drones;
  - **Drowned Bangalore:** the flood from Season 2 that never drained;
  - **Frozen Bangalore:** the ice age that never happened in your world;
  - **Ruined Bangalore:** empty, overgrown, a hundred years after everyone left;
  - **Echoes of the old seasons:** Phase 1's Bangalore, and even flashes of New Bangalore's neon.
- **Shifts happen without warning at first,** then you learn to read the signs: a shimmer in the air, a ringing sound, a colour cast creeping in.
- **The reactor** sits at the centre, in every reality at once: the one thing that never changes. It's where the season ends.
- **No gore or radiation sickness.** It's played as reality bending, not as a realistic nuclear disaster.

### Ideas for how it plays
- **Reality shifts as a mechanic:**
  - a ledge that's too high in one reality is a staircase in another;
  - a locked door in the future stands open in the ruins;
  - a river you can't cross in the swamp is a bridge in the medieval fort.
  - You wait for the right reality, or later force a shift yourself.
- **The phase anchor (the season's tool):**
  - built from the meteor core;
  - early on it holds you in place during a shift, so you don't get carried off into the wrong reality;
  - later it lets you choose which reality to shift into.
  - You keep it afterwards.
- **Things that follow you across shifts:** some enemies and some people come through with you, and some don't. Lose a pursuer by shifting away from them.
- **People you knew:**
  - versions of Phase 1 characters in different realities: a medieval Bhaskar, a far-future Velvet, a prehistoric Keeper;
  - some remember you, and some never met you.
- **A stability meter** for the whole city, shown on the phone: it drops each week as the shifts get faster and wilder, until the finale at the reactor.

### The villain: the reactor, awake (decided)
- The reactor at the Kaveri facility takes in the meteor core's power and **becomes sentient**.
- It's the one thing that exists in every reality at once. Shifting the city between realities is how it's learning: it pulls in each new version of Bangalore to study it, and keeps the pieces it likes.
- The more it learns, the more it wants to keep, and Bangalore's own reality is the next one on its list to be filed away.
- It speaks through anything electrical in every reality: a radio in the medieval fort, a hologram in the future, a buzzing street lamp in the ruins. Its voice changes to fit each one.

### Still to plan
- Chapters, bosses (one per reality would suit it), skins, the map trace and the trailer.
- **The build:**
  - the season engine counts 15 seasons and loops (`seasonInfo`, `SEASON_NAMES`, `SEASON_FILES`), so Phase 3 means letting it go past 15;
  - each reality is a reskin of the same street layout, with its own buildings, ground, sky, props and people, swapped as a set when the world shifts;
  - starting from the Phase 1 Bangalore code keeps that manageable, but it's still the biggest build in Phase 3.
